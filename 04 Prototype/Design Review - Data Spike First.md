## Problem Statement

NidhiNetra is an inspection-targeting system for MPLADS, built for SIH 2026 problem statement SIH26102 (MoSPI). District Authorities must physically inspect at least 10 percent of works under implementation every year, and nothing tells them which 10 percent. NidhiNetra ranks that already-mandated queue.

This session reviewed the build plan at `.claude/plan/nidhinetra-app-build.md` before six agent workstreams start against it.

## What Makes This Cool

The quota meter. The product thesis rendered as a physical bar: 168 works in the filter, the ten percent obligation falling at rank 17, everything past the cutoff drawn as hairline outline only. It makes a legal obligation visible as a line on a screen. That is the differentiator, more than the ML.

## Constraints

- Demo must run with wifi physically off (CP6), which forces a committed snapshot rather than a live fetch.
- No labelled fraud ground truth exists, so output is always "risk flags for human review," never "detected fraud."
- Internal round date unknown. Plan runs roughly 9 to 10 working days and cannot be scheduled against a deadline nobody has.
- Claude Design session limit reached, so further visual iteration must happen in React.

## Premises

Five premises tested. Three failed against live evidence.

- **P1. The MPLADS REST API makes data acquisition low-risk. FALSE.** Live probes on 2026-09-01: `getStateData` returns 36 states (a dropdown lookup); `getTenureData` returns `[]` for every payload tried; `getTilesData` and `getMpNameByStates` return ZK framework HTML, not JSON; `getReviewDetailsByWork` returns four citizen reviews and ignores its parameters entirely. Zero of the nine product-critical fields in contract 3.1 (`work_id`, `state`, `work_category`, `sanctioned_amount_inr`, `expenditure_amount_inr`, `sanction_date`, `completion_status`, `implementing_agency`, `vendor_name`) are confirmed retrievable. The Logbook's HIGH to LOW risk downgrade was not earned and is reverted.
- **P2. Data acquisition can run as a parallel Wave 1 workstream. FALSE.** It is a precondition. If A1 fails, A2 scores nothing real and A5 demos fixtures. CP1 is the project's single point of failure and the plan schedules its discovery during the parallel phase rather than before it.
- **P3. Code in `05 App/` yields a deliverable repo. FALSE.** `git rev-parse --show-toplevel` returns `/Users/krish/Desktop/study/project`. SIH is not its own repo; it shares a git history with WorldPolicy, MoodTune, disasterman and unrelated deleted files. There is no clone URL to hand a judge.
- **P4. The fund-flow graph earns its place. UNRESOLVED.** PRD.md:40 states the graph is "structurally meaningless below national scale." The rendered design filters to Bihar, Odisha and Jharkhand. PRD.md:82 names the graph as one of only two mitigations for the saturated-archetype risk. Load-bearing for differentiation, and only honest at a scale the demo does not run at.
- **P5. Port to React now. HOLDS.** Forced by the Claude Design limit rather than chosen, but sound. The `.dc.html` becomes a read-only reference.

The only genuine asset from the API work: `WORK_RECOM_DTL_ID` values (162799, 161939, 118396) prove work-level records exist and are numerically addressable. A thread to pull, not a data source.

## Approaches Considered

### Approach A: Data Spike First (chosen)
Halt all streams. One agent, one job: 500 real work records carrying the 3.1 fields, by any rung. Nothing else starts until CP1 passes. **Cost S. Outcome risk HIGH.** Cheap to run, and it may well come back empty. That is the point of running it first.

### Approach B: Ship the plan as written
Wave 1 as planned, A1 hunts data while A2 and A5 build on fixtures. Rejected: bets five streams on a premise live probing just contradicted, and surfaces failure at CP4 with four days sunk.

### Approach C: Curated corpus, cut the fund-flow graph
Build 500 to 2,000 curated rows from whichever rungs work; cut M5 because it cannot be honest at that scale. Not rejected, deferred. Becomes the plan if A returns empty.

## Recommended Approach

**A.** One day of certainty before committing five streams. It discards nothing and refuses to build on an unverified foundation. C stays available on day two, and A produces the evidence needed to choose C without guessing.

## Open Questions

- **Internal round date.** Blocks the entire calendar. Nothing else can be sequenced until this is known.
- **P4, the graph, and what the spike actually settles.** CP1 passes on the four fatal fields, none of which are `implementing_agency` or `vendor_name`. So a *passing* spike can still leave P4 open. Be precise about the three outcomes: agency and vendor present across 8-plus states means M5 survives; agency and vendor absent means M5 is cut, no further debate; agency and vendor present but only across two or three states means M5 is technically renderable and analytically hollow, and that is the case that needs your judgement rather than a rule. Note also that 8 of 36 states is not "national scale" as PRD.md:40 uses the term, so even the good outcome is a weaker claim than the PRD currently makes.
- **The ₹1,729.61 cr and ₹5.93 cr figures** still need confirming against primary sources before appearing on a slide.
- **CP1 is deliberately raised above PRD M1.** M1 asks for "500 real records, schema-valid," which a schema permitting nulls would pass with empty amounts. This review raises the bar to the four fatal fields plus the 8-state sampling frame. That is an intentional tightening, not a contradiction, and PRD M1 should be updated to match.

## Success Criteria

CP1 as written in the build plan says "500 or more real records." That is too loose to settle anything. Two additions.

**Per-field verdict, because partial success is the likely outcome.** Contract 3.1 defines fourteen fields. Nine carry the product, and they are not equally load-bearing:

| Field | If missing |
|---|---|
| `work_id` | Fatal. Nothing is addressable. |
| `sanctioned_amount_inr` | Fatal. The cost-outlier detector is the primary flag. |
| `work_category` | Fatal. No category means no peer group, and peer group is mandatory on every flagged record. |
| `state` | Fatal. No peer group, no filtering. |
| `completion_status` | Degradable. Stalled-work detection weakens; cost outliers still fire. |
| `expenditure_amount_inr` | Degradable. Loses the expenditure-mismatch flag, keeps the rest. |
| `sanction_date` | Degradable. Needed for stalled-work detection ("sanctioned 14 months ago, zero spend"). Without it that flag dies, cost outliers survive. |
| `implementing_agency` | Degradable *for the table*, fatal for M5. No agency means no fund-flow graph. |
| `vendor_name` | Degradable *for the table*, fatal for M5. Least likely field to be exposed publicly. |

Four fatal fields, five degradable. **CP1 passes on the four fatal fields alone.** Missing agency and vendor does not stop the product, it cuts M5, which is Approach C.

**Sampling frame, because 500 rows from one state settles nothing.** CP1 additionally requires records spanning **at least 8 states and 25 districts**. Without this, P4 is exactly where it started and the claim that the spike settles the graph question is false.

**Owner, arbiter and clock.** Two distinct jobs. **You** do the capture, tonight, with a browser and the network tab open, because it needs a human clicking through a stateful UI. **One agent** does the bulk pull afterwards, using whatever request you captured. One working day total from the moment the capture starts. **You are the arbiter**, not the agent: at the end of that day you write A-continues or C-switch into the Logbook. No extension without a second explicit decision, because "one more day" repeated three times is how this kind of spike quietly eats a week.

**Validator exemption.** `contracts/validate.py` does not exist yet and Wave 0 is halted by this approach, so "validator green" is circular. During the spike, CP1 is judged by a manual field-presence check against the table above. The validator gets written in Wave 0, after the spike returns.

## Distribution Plan

Local demo, run from a cloned repo with wifi off. `make demo` starts FastAPI over the committed Parquet snapshot and serves the Next.js frontend. No deployment target, no CI, no registry.

**One trap.** "Runs with wifi off" is only true after dependencies are installed. A fresh clone still needs `npm install` and `uv sync`, both networked. CP6 must therefore be verified on a machine that has already installed once, and the dry run in CP7 must not start from a clean clone. If the demo has to survive a truly cold machine, commit `node_modules` or ship a pre-built image, and decide that before CP6 rather than during it.

**Resolving the clone contradiction.** Two different questions were tangled here. *Can a judge clone and inspect the repo?* Premise 3 answers no today, and step 1 of Next Steps fixes it. *Does the demo run from a cold clone with wifi off?* No, and it never will unless dependencies are vendored. Pick one: either CP7 runs on your own already-installed machine and you say so honestly, or you vendor `node_modules` and pre-build. Decide before CP6, not during it.

## The Assignment

**Two targets, not one, and the second matters more.**

The obvious move is to open one work's detail page and capture the request behind it. Do that, but understand what it does and does not buy you. One detail request gives you one work. The three known IDs (162799, 161939, 118396) are non-contiguous, so you cannot iterate a counter to 500. **The request you actually need is the one that returns a LIST of works with their IDs.** Detail-by-ID is worthless without enumeration.

**Expect rung 1 to fail, and know why.** Two of the four dead endpoints returned ZK framework HTML (`/zkau/`, `zk.wcs`). ZK renders server-side against stateful desktop IDs, so the work-detail view may have no JSON request to intercept at all, only ZK update fragments bound to a session. If that is what you find, rung 1 is dead by construction rather than by bad luck, and you move to rung 2 (Playwright driving the real UI and scraping the rendered DOM) without spending the afternoon hunting for an API that was never there.

Concretely, tonight:
1. Open `https://mplads.mospi.gov.in` with the network tab recording, filtered to XHR and Doc.
2. Navigate to any state, then to a works listing. **Capture the listing request.** Note whether the response is JSON or a ZK fragment.
3. Open one work's detail. Capture that request too.
4. Write both findings to the Logbook, including the negative result if the responses are ZK fragments. A negative result recorded is worth more than a positive one assumed.

If the listing request returns JSON, **rung 1 is alive** and worth a day. That is all it proves. Whether the response actually carries the four fatal fields, spans eight states, and yields 500 rows is the next question, not a settled one. If it returns ZK, you are on rung 2 and should say so out loud rather than retrying rung 1.
