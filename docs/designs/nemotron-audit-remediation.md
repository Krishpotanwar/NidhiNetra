## Problem Statement

NidhiNetra is built, deployed, and live end to end (Vercel frontend, Render backend, real MPLADS data). An independent audit (`nemotronreview.md`, project root) reviewed the whole codebase and found real correctness bugs, two of them severe enough to break a demo in front of a domain-expert judging panel. This session's headline finding was independently spot-verified against the source, not taken on faith: `05-App/pipeline/src/nidhinetra_pipeline/ingest/mplads_adapter.py:413` assigns the raw field `IDA_NAME` (the Implementing District Authority) to a field the entire app calls `implementing_agency`. `IA_NAME` (the actual executing agency) exists in the same source data and is never read.

The task: decide which of the audit's 23 findings to fix and which of its 13 recommended features to add, in what order, without a fixed deadline.

## What Makes This Cool

The audit itself is the opportunity. SIH26102 is MoSPI's own problem statement — the judging panel plausibly includes people who administer MPLADS and know clause 4.5.2 cold. Most teams building on this same public dataset almost certainly have the identical IA_NAME/IDA_NAME confusion and don't know it. Catching it and showing the catch, on screen, converts an outside audit finding blocker bugs from a liability into the strongest trust signal a compliance tool can offer a domain-expert audience.

## Constraints

- No fixed deadline; the plan must be buildable incrementally in priority order, so stopping at any point still means the highest-value work shipped first.
- Not a rewrite: `IA_NAME` and `VENDOR_ID` already flow through the raw ingest pipeline and are dropped or mislabeled downstream, not missing from source.
- Zero real inspection outcomes exist yet, so no fix may depend on labelled ground truth that doesn't exist.

## Premises

1. Fixing entity mislabeling and false graph paths matters more right now than any new feature — agreed.
2. Doing nothing is a real risk, not hypothetical: the app currently claims a legal 10%-compliance behavior the code doesn't deliver — agreed.
3. This is not a rewrite; the needed raw fields already exist in the pipeline — agreed.

All three confirmed by the user without revision.

- **Existing tools worth porting (pattern, not platform):** OCCRP's Aleph/FollowTheMoney project enforces exactly the discipline F-02 violates (edges only via an explicit shared identifier, never inferred adjacency) — port as a `networkx` MultiDiGraph with a mandatory `evidence_id` field, not the whole Aleph stack. The `apportionment` PyPI package (legislative seat-apportionment math: largest remainder, D'Hondt, Sainte-Laguë) solves the same rounding problem as splitting a 10% quota correctly across districts — directly applicable to F-03/F-04.
- **One-weekend priority:** fix F-03/F-04 first, not F-01/F-02. It is the cheapest of the three blockers (pure query/aggregation logic, no schema or pipeline change), sits directly under the product's one-sentence thesis, and is the single most likely first question a MoSPI panel asks: "walk me through how the 10% is enforced." ## Approaches Considered

### Approach A: Fix the thesis only
F-03/F-04 only: quota computed per District Authority (via `apportionment`'s largest-remainder method for correct rounding), rank and cutoff aligned to one population. Replace the global quota badge with a live per-district tracker. Effort S (2-3 days), risk low, no schema changes. Rejected as the final scope (see below) but kept as the guaranteed floor if time runs out mid-implementation — nothing in Approach B is wasted if work stops after A.

### Approach B: Full correctness — everything blocker-severity (chosen)
A, plus rebuild the fund-flow graph as evidence-backed edges (F-02, via the ported Aleph/FollowTheMoney pattern: `networkx` MultiDiGraph, mandatory `evidence_id`), plus the real F-01 fix (separate `implementing_district_authority` and `implementing_agency` fields everywhere — API, pipeline, frontend labels — after auditing what silently depends on `IDA_NAME`'s smaller cardinality), plus dropping the circular "100% precision" / "2.48x lift" claims (F-08) and the client-settable control-group flag (F-09). Effort L (1-2+ weeks). Closes every Blocker-severity finding.

### Approach C: Turn the audit into the demo
A's fix, packaged as a live "we audit our own tool" moment: on-screen before/after of the graph collapsing under the evidence-ID requirement, `IDA_NAME` vs `IA_NAME` named explicitly. Not chosen as a separate track — its presentation idea is folded into Approach B's F-02 work (see Next Steps) rather than run as its own narrower scope, since B already includes the underlying graph rebuild.

## Recommended Approach

**B**, in this order: F-03/F-04 first (cheapest, most central to the thesis, lowest risk), then F-02 (built and packaged as Approach C's before/after demo moment, not hidden), then F-01 (highest ripple risk, done last with a dependency audit first), then the F-08/F-09 copy and API changes (cheap, no reason to defer). This sequencing means the project is demo-safe after every single step, not only at the end.

**F-08 — not applicable to the live app.** Every "precision"/"lift" hit in the frontend and `strings.json` is a code comment about why rank must be server-computed, numeric-formatting precision (decimal places), or a historical dev-log entry — never a rendered "100% precision" or "2.48x lift" figure in the deployed UI. That claim lives only in pitch/deck material, outside this plan's scope. No code change needed for F-08; check the slides separately.

**Both prior open questions are already answered by code that ships today.** `policy.py`'s `quota_for()` already does `max(1, ceil(population_n * 10 / 100))` — "at least 10%" mathematically forces rounding up, and `max(1, ...)` already floors a small district at 1 (and a 0-work district at 0). No external clause-interpretation needed; it just needs to run once per District Authority instead of once nationally.

**Correction: drop the `apportionment` dependency.** Apportionment methods solve a different problem — fairly redistributing a *fixed total* among groups when naive per-group quotas don't sum to it (legislative-seat allocation). Clause 4.5.2 has no fixed national total to redistribute; each authority's 10% stands independently. `quota_for()`, called per district, is already the complete answer.

**Correction: drop the `networkx` dependency too.** It's declared in `pipeline/pyproject.toml` but never imported anywhere — a phantom dependency already. It wouldn't help anyway: the path-validity check has to run in the TypeScript frontend, which can't use a Python graph library. The real fix is smaller: add a `work_ids` list to each edge in `build_graph.py` (and `contracts/fund_flow_graph.schema.json`, a cross-boundary shape), and change `allVendorConcentrations`'s two-hop walk to require the MP→Agency and Agency→Vendor edges share a real `work_id` before crediting that MP to that vendor. Plain `Set` intersection, no library either side.

**New finding: F-01 is more than a field rename, two ways.**
1. `IA_NAME` doesn't live on the Sanctioned tile `mplads_adapter.py` reads today — it rides the per-payment-event Expenditure tile, same as `VENDOR_NAME`. A real fix needs an `IA_NAME` rollup parallel to `rollup_expenditure()`'s vendor selection (modal name, ties broken alphabetically) — inheriting F-05's multi-vendor ambiguity, now for agencies too. The 31,953 works with zero expenditure events will correctly show no agency at all post-fix, not a wrong one — more honest, but a visible change worth expecting, not discovering live.
2. Real `IA_NAME` has ~5,868 distinct values against today's ~733 (mislabeled `IDA_NAME`) values — roughly 8x more fund-flow-graph middle nodes, compounding the audit's own F-17 finding (`allVendorConcentrations` already runs ~319.5 million edge checks on the smaller graph today). Fold F-17's server-side-aggregation fix into F-02's rebuild now, rather than hitting this as a surprise once F-01 lands.

**Good news for sequencing: F-03/F-04 does not need to wait on F-01.** Today's `works.implementing_agency` column is already populated from `IDA_NAME` — mislabeled in name, but already holding District-Authority-shaped values (`District Collector`, `District Magistrate`, etc.). Grouping by it for per-district quota is already correct in substance; F-01 later renaming the column is a mechanical follow-up, not new logic. The chosen order (F-03/F-04 → F-02 → F-01 → F-08/F-09) holds.

**No frontend filter depends on `IDA_NAME`'s cardinality.** `GET /api/works/facets` facets on `state`, `year`, `category`, `flag` only — there is no agency filter today. F-01's real ripple risk is the graph-size interaction above, not filter breakage.

## Open Questions

- Watch the fund-flow graph's size after the F-01 remap (~8x more middle-tier nodes) and make sure F-02's server-side aggregation (folded in above) actually absorbs it before F-01 ships.

## Success Criteria

- No completed work can occupy an annual under-implementation quota slot (F-04's acceptance test).
- Every visible fund-flow edge returns at least one real supporting work ID; a constructed test proves two unrelated works sharing only an authority cannot form a path (F-02's acceptance test).
- A sampled raw record shows the same `IDA_NAME` and `IA_NAME` labels in the UI as in the source, separately (F-01's acceptance test).
- No visible metric claims "precision" or "lift" without a stated population and method; the app never claims verified inspection accuracy while zero real outcomes exist.

## R-06 Pulled Forward: Entity Alias Review

Decided during a second `/office-hours` pass, 2026-09-13, after the Verification Pass above. Presented as three approaches (A: fix identity key only; B: full review-queue feature; C: fix now, log ambiguous cases for later, no UI). Recommended C; the user chose B.

**Root cause, confirmed directly:** `build_graph.py`'s `_node_id("Vendor", vendor_name)` keys every vendor node by a slugified NAME STRING, never by `VENDOR_ID`. F-05's own evidence: 806 exact vendor name strings map to more than one `VENDOR_ID` (max 10), and "DINESH KUMAR" alone resolves to 6 IDs exactly, 9 case-insensitively. `_ExpenditureRollup.vendor()` already picks one name by raw event-count majority, alphabetical tiebreak — an undocumented automatic merge, exactly what R-06 exists to replace with a reviewed one.

**Open question Approach B creates, and how it's resolved:** this app deliberately has no authentication (an audit-endorsed choice for the judge-demo profile). "Who clicks confirm" therefore has no verified answer. Resolution: follow the exact precedent `InspectionOutcomeRequest.inspector_id` already sets — a self-reported, free-text `reviewed_by` field, not a verified identity. Not a compromise invented for this feature; it is how this project already handles the same absence everywhere else it appears.

**Inherited risk, flagged directly (outside-voice review, 2026-09-14):** that precedent is itself an open, un-fixed audit finding — `nemotronreview.md`'s F-10 names the same self-reported-identity gap on `inspector_id` as a real weakness, not a solved problem. R-06 knowingly reuses a pattern the audit has already flagged rather than inventing a new gap, which is a defensible reuse-over-novelty call, but it is not risk-free: `reviewed_by` on `entity_alias_reviews` inherits F-10 exactly, and fixing F-10 later (real auth) means touching both tables' reviewer field, not just one. ## Fund-Flow Rendering: Sigma.js Replaces Hand-Rolled SVG

Decided during `/plan-eng-review`, 2026-09-14. `GraphView.tsx` was reported "glitchy and messy" — traced to a real cause, not a 2D-vs-3D one: a hand-rolled, one-row-per-node SVG layout (`ROW_HEIGHT = 30px`, no virtualization) that scales unbounded with node count — `28*2 + 16854*30` ≈ 505,000px tall unfiltered, ~164,778px even after the existing 3-MP filter (per an existing `strings.json` amendment-log note). Thousands of raw SVG DOM nodes in a canvas that tall, recalculating opacity on every hover, is the actual glitchiness.

3D was considered and rejected: on a normal (non-VR, non-stereoscopic) screen, research shows 3D network graphs lose the depth cues that sometimes help 3D elsewhere while keeping full occlusion and edge-crossing costs — worse for a connectivity-reading task, not better. Separately, `nemotronreview.md` explicitly names "a national all-entities Sankey or hairball graph" as a feature to avoid, warning that a dramatic visualization here "would make the false-path and entity-semantic problems more persuasive visually, which increases rather than reduces risk" — exactly the risk a premium 3D render would add. `DESIGN.md`'s existing restrained aesthetic commitment is for the same reason.

**Chosen fix:** swap to Sigma.js (WebGL-batched rendering, purpose-built for thousands of nodes — the clear 2026 recommendation over Cytoscape/vis-network/D3 at this scale). Stays 2D; fixes the actual cause. Lands after F-02 (needs the corrected, evidence-backed, likely-smaller graph data) — its own step, not bundled into F-02's PR.
